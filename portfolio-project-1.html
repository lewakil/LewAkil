Club Swan Pre-paid Card → NFT “Digital Twin” System

Project Summary:

Designed and delivered a system that minted an ERC-721 NFT as a “digital twin” for each purchased prepaid card, connecting real-world purchases to on-chain proof of ownership and enabling digital-first experiences through verifiable tokenization.

My Role:

  • Technical Lead + Hands-on Engineer: Co-owned end-to-end architecture decisions, delivery planning, and implementation support across blockchain + app teams.
  • Architecture & System Design: Mapped purchase events → mint authorization → on-chain mint → metadata publication, aligning contracts, services, and operational controls.
  • Feasibility & Integration Research: Evaluated platforms/tooling (e.g., OpenSea, IPFS) and documented constraints, tradeoffs, and recommended integration patterns.
  • Engineering Execution: Reviewed PRs, contributed code when needed, and unblocked complex implementation issues across smart contract + integration layers.
  • Documentation & Alignment: Authored architecture decisions, implementation specs, and workflows to keep delivery consistent and extensible.
  • Delivery Leadership: Coordinated across blockchain, backend, frontend, and QA; translated progress into executive-ready updates and de-risked milestones.

Architecture:

  • Core Flow: Prepaid card purchase triggers a controlled mint request; system validates eligibility/authorization before minting to prevent duplication and fraud.
  • Smart Contracts: Implemented ERC-721 minting patterns using OpenZeppelin; structured token metadata for marketplace interoperability.
  • Minting Integrity: Designed idempotent mint logic and correlation identifiers to ensure “one purchase → one NFT,” even under retries or partial failures.
  • Metadata & Storage: Evaluated IPFS storage and metadata formats to support portability and compatibility with marketplace ingestion requirements.
  • Wallet & UX Foundations: Defined wallet ownership and access assumptions for viewing/transferring NFTs as the digital counterpart to the physical card.
  • Security & Controls: Considered authorization boundaries, mint permissions, and verification controls to protect the minting process and contract interactions.

Conclusion / Outcome:

Delivered a production-ready architecture that reliably connected prepaid card purchases to ERC-721 minting, enabling verifiable “digital twin” ownership and marketplace-compatible metadata. The system was designed for real-world operations — emphasizing mint integrity (one purchase → one NFT), secure authorization boundaries, and portable storage patterns (e.g., IPFS) — providing a scalable foundation that could be extended for future card programs, loyalty experiences, or additional digital benefits tied to verified ownership.
